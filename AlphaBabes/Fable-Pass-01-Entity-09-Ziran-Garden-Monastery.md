THE WORLD'S PHYSICS SHAPE EVERYTHING: Kepler-442b — 1.3 times Earth's gravity under a dim ORANGE-AMBER star. Heavy gravity means all structures are LOW, WIDE and massive-shouldered: nothing tall, nothing slender, no soaring towers — architecture like a settled old orchard, hugging the mountain. The dim warm light means the native forest is DEEP-HUED: foliage in dark teal, blue-green and near-black olive (dim-star plants harvest every photon), with the monastery's cultivated gardens glowing in the lighter greens and golds of tended, sun-favored breeds. Perpetual thin silver MIST drifts through everything — this is a world of muffled distances where sound matters more than sight.

THE MONASTERY: a terraced sanctuary grown as much as built — the Zĭrán train LIVING TREES into architecture: broad low halls whose columns are living trunks, roofed with overlapping tiles of pale cultivated bark and cushions of deliberate moss in embroidered patterns. Walls are woven lattice of living branches, translucent silk-paper panels glowing amber from lamps within. Everything connects by wide, gentle RAMPS with grip-textured stone (four walking limbs need footing, never steps). Doorways are broad horizontal ellipses, low and wide, curtained with strands of wooden beads that CHIME — every threshold makes a note, because this is an acoustic civilization: wind-harps in the eaves, tuned water-drip vessels in the gardens, hollow resonating gourds hung in the trees — the whole monastery is a gentle instrument played by the weather.

THE TEA-COURTYARD (heart of the fable): a circular court of rain-polished dark stone, open to the sky for skin-basking (the Zĭrán part-feed on light — show two monks resting flank-up in the amber sun like contented solar panels). At its center a shallow raised hearth-ring with a carved wooden tea-pavilion: low tables at hexapod height (knee-high to a human), wooden tea bowls, a slow iron kettle, steam mixing with the mist. Woven mantles hang on a rail. The mood: a place where nothing has ever been hurried.

THE SCROLL-LIBRARY TERRACE: honeycomb shelves of dark wood holding thousands of ROLLED BARK-PAPER SCROLLS, protected under deep eaves; reading-hollows in the floor where a Zĭrán settles sphinx-like, scroll pinned open by grip-pads; small oil lamps with amber flames; one wall given to star-charts — including a prominently displayed, lovingly hand-copied chart of a certain distant yellow star, annotated in flowing chord-notation script.

GARDENS & SETTING: stepped terraces of vegetable plots, tea-shrub rows and seed-nurseries descending the mountainside; a slow stream threaded through every terrace in carved channels, turning small prayer-wheels as it falls; beyond and below, an ocean of dark-teal forest canopy under drifting mist; above, the huge dim orange sun hanging soft-edged in a pale apricot sky, and — faintly visible even in daylight — two small moons.

LIGHT & MOOD: warm amber daylight diffused through mist, long soft shadows, lamplight answering from within the silk-paper walls; the whole image should feel like the inside of a held breath — serenity, patience, and the quiet confidence of a civilization that chose gardens over empires. Painterly realistic detail, hard-science-fiction plausibility in every material. Reference sheet: crisp focus, no dramatic action, no text labels in the image.
