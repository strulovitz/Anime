SCENE 1 — The Alpha in the Void

Image prompt:

Exterior wide shot of the interstellar mothership "Alpha" drifting through deep space, dwarfed by the star-dusted blackness of the Milky Way band behind it. The ship is built like a colossal six-barrel Gatling gun: six full-sized submarine hulls of dull reddish copper arranged in a ring around a seventh, hollow central cylinder that runs the ship's whole length. Short pressurized tunnel bridges (like submarine conning towers) connect each neighboring hull. At the bow: an enormous shallow geodesic dish, a hemisphere of hundreds of triangular graphene panels with carbon-nanotube edge struts, glinting faintly — the hydrogen-collecting funnel of a Bussard ramjet, with the hollow central cylinder as its long solenoid throat. A few triangles nearest the center are subtly outlined hatches (missile ports). Almost the entire hull between bow and stern is buried under a lumpy, tied-down quilt of huge sealed ice-filled bags, like giant white sandbags lashed with cargo netting — radiation shielding — giving the mighty ship a humble, patchwork, hand-made warmth. At the stern: one large central exhaust and six smaller thruster nozzles glowing faint blue. The whole ship rotates slowly about its long axis. Lighting: cold starlight rim-light, one warm ember of a distant red dwarf star (Proxima Centauri) ahead. Mood: awe, loneliness, quiet courage. Camera: three-quarter front view from below, the dish catching the red star's light.

Narration (voiceover):

    "This is the Alpha. She left Earth twelve centuries ago — not the fastest ship ever dreamed, but the wisest. Six copper submarines ride around one hollow heart, wrapped in a blanket of ice against the killing rays of deep space. Her great dish drinks hydrogen from the void itself. She cannot stop, and she cannot turn back. And inside her, ten sleeping women carry everything humanity ever was — toward a small red star called Proxima Centauri."

Ken Burns: slow push-in from wide black space toward the dish, ending on the red star's glint.
SCENE 2 — Awakening

Image prompt:

Interior of the Alpha's cryogenic bay, intimate medium shot. MADIE — a beautiful Romanian woman in her early twenties, long dark-brown hair loose over her shoulders, warm brown eyes just opening, womanly curvy figure, wearing a fitted white-and-slate spacesuit with a small ROMANIA flag patch on the left shoulder and a chest name tag reading "MADIE", absolutely nothing on her head — sits up slowly from an opened cryo-pod, one hand pressed to the pod's rim. The pod is a sleek horizontal capsule, its glass canopy raised, inner surfaces still feathered with melting frost; thin wisps of cold vapor spill over the edge and pool on the deck. Around her, a curved row of nine more pods stretches into soft darkness, each glowing gently with amber status lights, frost on their glass, sleeping silhouettes inside. Through a small round porthole behind her, the dim coal-red light of Proxima Centauri paints one side of her face; warm interior maintenance lights paint the other. A small wheeled robot stands by her pod offering a thermal blanket with two gentle manipulator arms. Her expression: groggy wonder turning into quiet resolve — the look of someone waking from a 1,200-year sleep and remembering why. Copper bulkheads, honest rivets and pipes, everything worn but lovingly maintained. Camera: low, close, eye-level with her. Mood: tender, hushed, historic.

Narration:

    "Twelve hundred years. Empires rose and fell on the Earth they left behind — and the ten slept on, dreaming in the frost, while the ship's silent robots kept watch. Then, one morning older than any morning in history, the pods opened. Madie woke first — commander, engineer, daughter of Romania. She had crossed more time than any human being who ever lived. Her first thought was not fear. It was: we made it."

Ken Burns: start on the frosted row of pods, drift slowly onto Madie's opening eyes.

SCENE 4 — First Footfall

Image prompt:

Surface of exoplanet Proxima b, in the eternal twilight zone between the scorching day side and frozen night side. Rust-colored regolith plain under a huge, dim, coal-red sun (Proxima Centauri) sitting low on the horizon — it looks three times larger than Earth's sun but sullen and red. Across the deep-purple sky, shimmering green and violet auroras ripple — the afterglow of a recent stellar flare. In the foreground, a fresh line of boot prints presses into untouched rusty soil, leading to MADIE, standing in quiet triumph: Romanian, early 20s, long dark-brown hair moving softly, absolutely nothing on her head, curvy figure, white-and-slate spacesuit with ROMANIA shoulder patch and "MADIE" chest name tag, her suit's neck ring open and empty. Slightly behind her: ACE (lilac afro puffs visible through a clear glass bubble helmet, fist raised in celebration) and ROCKHOUND (dark-green bob through her clear helmet, deadpan but secretly moved), both in matching suits with USA and Russia flag patches. Behind them on a rise, their landed shuttle: a titanium mini-submarine standing vertically on four landing legs, Falcon-9 style, a thin heat-shimmer under it. A small six-wheeled rover and a fixed-wing drone rest nearby. Long soft shadows stretch toward the night side. Lighting: cinematic red-golden side light with aurora fill. Mood: Armstrong-moment reverence, the first footprints beyond the Solar System. Camera: low angle behind the footprints, heroes mid-frame, red sun and auroras dominating the sky.

Narration:

    "Proxima b. The nearest world of another sun — and the first to feel a human step. The air is thin and hostile, the red dwarf hurls flares that would strip an unlucky world bare. And yet — look. Footprints. Four billion years this dust waited, and no one ever came. Madie stood in the twilight between the burning day and the frozen night, and spoke the words into the log: 'We are far from home. And we are exactly where we belong.'"

Ken Burns: begin tight on the boot prints, pull back and up to reveal the figures, sun, and auroras.
SCENE 5 — First Life

Image prompt:

Inside a dark volcanic lava-tube cave on Proxima b. Intimate, sacred close shot. REAPER — Indian woman, early 20s, mint-green hair in a neat chignon visible through her clear glass bubble helmet, India flag patch, warm brown eyes brimming with tears of joy — kneels on the rocky floor, one gloved hand hovering, visibly trembling, just above a patch of alien lichen clinging to the cave wall. The lichen: crusted rosettes of silver-blue and deep teal, edged with hair-fine filaments, faintly bioluminescent, throwing a soft cyan glow onto her glove. Around it, threads of pale fungal mycelium vein the dark basalt, dotted with tiny glowing pinpoints like a buried constellation. Mineral crystals in the rock glitter in the beams of two suit-mounted lamps. Behind Reaper, slightly out of focus: MADIE (nothing on her head, long dark-brown hair, ROMANIA patch, "MADIE" name tag) crouching with a hand on Reaper's shoulder, awestruck, and DOC (pink box braids through a clear helmet, France patch) holding a sample case, forgetting to open it. The cave mouth far behind them glows faint red-violet with aurora light from the flare outside. Lighting: darkness pierced by warm suit lamps and the cold living glow of the lichen — the two lights meeting on Reaper's face. Mood: the most important scientific discovery in human history, rendered as a quiet, almost religious moment. Camera: close over the lichen, looking up slightly at Reaper's face.

Narration:

    "They had come to escape a flare — and found, sheltering in the same cave, the answer to humanity's oldest question. Life. Not tall, not proud — a patch of lichen no bigger than a hand, glowing softly in the dark, enduring the fury of its star underground, just as they did. Reaper's hand trembled above it. No one spoke. Somewhere behind their eyes, every scientist who ever lived was weeping. We were never alone. Life finds a way — here, and everywhere."

Ken Burns: start on the glowing lichen, drift up slowly to Reaper's tear-lit eyes.
