Single full-frame interior-exterior: the OBSERVATION CUPOLA of the manned submarine hull of the Alpha — the quietest room on the ship, and its one engineered indulgence: the only true windows aboard, and the crew knows exactly what they cost. The space: a small blister of thick FUSED-SILICA panes in a machined titanium frame — the ISS cupola's great-granddaughter — each pane a laminated stack rated against micrometeoroids, and around the glass, its armored debris SHUTTERS retracted like opened petals, their actuator rods visible: engineering honesty on display, because these windows are never open toward the ram direction, never used for navigation, and never apologized for — they exist for the crew's souls, nothing else, and the ship's designers decided that was a valid subsystem. Beyond the glass, filling the upper two-thirds of frame: the raw magnificent STARFIELD rendered with astronomical honesty — thousands of stars in true magnitudes and colors, the Milky Way's dust lanes arching corner to corner, no lens flares, no twinkle, the terrifying clean stillness of vacuum — and low near the frame's edge, one star very slightly brighter than its neighbors: Proxima. The destination. Twelve hundred years patient. Inside, the lower third, lit only by starlight and one shielded RED night-lamp by the hatch — red to protect dark-adapted eyes, dim to protect the view: a few worn cushions with folded blankets on the padded deck, each cushion subtly shaped by its owner across lifetimes — and one blanket thrown back, still dented. Someone was here minutes ago. A compact motorized TELESCOPE on a hull mount, tracking silently; a tablet running a sky atlas dimmed to red mode, a thin line marking the ship's own trajectory across the constellations; and open on the vacated cushion, the room's real treasure: a private paper NOTEBOOK — constellation sketches spanning centuries in one slowly evolving hand, the same handwriting turning young again and again as its owner does — and on the current page, one star circled many times over, pen pressed hard enough to dent the paper. A vacuum flask and one cup wait beside it, cap off, still steaming faintly. Lighting: starlight as key — cold, faint, absolutely honest — the red lamp a single ember at the frame's edge. Mood: the room where ten immortal women keep re-deciding, night after night, body after body, that the voyage is worth it — and keep deciding yes.
