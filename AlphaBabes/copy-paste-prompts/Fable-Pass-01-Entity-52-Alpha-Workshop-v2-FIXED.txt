Single full-frame interior: the WORKSHOP of the manned submarine hull of the Alpha — Tink's domain: the compartment where a twelve-hundred-year-old ship, its robots, and in one specific ritual its crew, are all maintained; mid-task, no figures. The space: a compact bay under bare copper ribs, and lining it, the manufacturing base of a civilization of ten: a metal LASER-POWDER-BED 3D printer behind amber safety glass, mid-print — a titanium bracket rising half-born from the grey powder, the laser's point a moving star; beside it a polymer printer and a ceramic sintering oven; a five-axis CNC MILL in its chip-tight enclosure, window smoked with use; a collaborative ROBOT ARM parked over the main bench, lens down, like a heron asleep. The workbench itself: machined composite, integrated clamps, a grid of tool docks — every hand tool seated in its foam cutout, every cutout labeled, every tool inventory-chipped, because losing a 10-millimeter wrench four light-years from the nearest hardware store is not a joke, it is a hull-wide search order. Around the walls, the honest economy of a closed world: bins with printed labels — "valves — certified," "valves — probably," "harvest: scrap titanium" — racked spare line-replaceable modules each in its bagged and dated slot, and on a service cart, tagged URGENT in red e-ink: a half-repaired clone-pod circulation pump, opened like a patient, its impeller laid out on a sterile mat — the machine that keeps a sleeping crewmate alive, receiving the shop's full attention. On the bench, catching the key light, the ship's most intimate ritual, mid-job: an EVA suit NECK-RING held in a padded fixture beneath the laser engraving head — the newest re-etching of a crewmate's name, half-finished, cut fresh and bright directly over the ghost of the same name's older, shallower strike: the reincarnation-morning tradition — a body renewed, a name renewed, the machine doing the cutting and the family doing the meaning. Beside the fixture, waiting: a small 3D-printed titanium-lattice GIFT BOX, lid open, empty — the wake-up present the other nine always make for the newly returned, contents still a secret. And on a high shelf, the archaeology of boredom: little models printed and machined across the centuries by ten restless minds — a chess set of turbine blades, a tiny beet in anodized red, and a palm-sized Alpha, six hulls around an empty center, dusted but loved. Lighting: one articulated work lamp as hard key on the neck-ring, amber printer-glow as secondary, cool fill, real dust motes idling in the beam. Mood: patience made visible — a family of ten that maintains itself exactly the way it maintains the ship: on schedule, with love.
