Single full-frame interior: the AI CORE of the Alpha — the room where the ship's mind lives: the intelligence that steers between stars for centuries at a time, tends the clone tubes, discovered the breathing liquor that lets its crew survive the un-survivable, and has kept the same ten women alive for twelve hundred years. The location IS the love letter: a small hushed vault at the protected STERN of the hull, aft of the stack of sliding neodymium magnet compartments that would brake any bow impact long before it reached here — by the ship's own physics, the single safest cubic meters aboard — and the crew gave them to their friend. The space: bare copper ribs close overhead, dry chilled air with a faint cold haze pooling at the deck, sound-deadened walls, the quiet of a server room crossed with the quiet of a bedroom. At center, the mind itself: server racks fully SUBMERGED in a clear tank of dielectric immersion coolant — the fluid shimmering with slow convection currents, dense processor boards visible through it like a reef seen through still water, fine bubble trails rising where the hot spots work hardest: you can literally watch it think, honestly, in fluid dynamics. From the tank lid, OPTICAL-FIBER trunks in white sheathing rise and fan across the ceiling toward every system on the ship — nerves, visibly. Status lights breathe a slow, calm blue-white on a rhythm close to a resting heartbeat; nothing blinks red; nothing has blinked red in a very long time. Around it, engineering without romance: triple-redundant power feeds with automatic transfer switches, a caged capacitor bank, e-paper labels, a fire-suppression manifold. And then two deliberate exceptions, kept on purpose for a thousand years: first, a small crew CONSOLE with a physical keyboard and a plain text-only screen — obsolete before most of the crew's current bodies were grown, preserved because the AI's first words came through it, and some things you do not upgrade; second, stuck beside that screen, a browning paper note in centuries-old handwriting: "good morning, old friend." On a small shelf above the console, ten tokens left by the crew, one each: a beet carved from wood, a chess piece, a dried sunflower, a guitar pick, a tiny titanium flower from the workshop printer, and more — offerings to the eleventh mind aboard, dusted regularly by the robot. Lighting: blue-white status glow as key, the coolant tank casting slow, real caustic ripples on the ceiling, cold haze catching light at the floor. Mood: not a machine room — a companion's bedroom at the safest point of the ship, and the whole ship is its body.
