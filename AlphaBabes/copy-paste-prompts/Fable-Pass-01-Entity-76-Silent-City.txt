Single full-frame establishing masterpiece, elegiac register: the SILENT CITY — the ten-thousand-year-dead metropolis around the Oracle Node, on a world whose civilization rose, flowered and vanished before humanity smelted bronze, seen from a rubble rise at dead noon under a white ashen sky. The world: grey — volcanic ash has fallen and re-fallen for a hundred centuries, softening everything into a single hushed monochrome, drifts lying against ruins like snow that never melts; a weak white sun, no wind at this hour, the stillness itself the subject. The city: architecture by a vanished species that was NOT humanoid and the ruins say so honestly — no stairs anywhere, only broad spiraling RAMPS; doorways low, wide and triple-arched, sized for bodies broader than tall; buildings of a once-beautiful pale alloy and fused ceramic, now pitted, ash-choked, their upper structures long collapsed, but their proportions still graceful under the burial — a city that was loved by its builders, every ruin a sentence in a language with no readers left. Rendered exactly to canon at the composition's heart, small and precious: the ORACLE NODE — a power-starved, dust-caked projector pillar of pitted pale alloy, waist-high, its tended cables running across the ash to a sad brave array of salvaged, cracked solar panels propped at hopeful angles; above the pillar, dim and FLICKERING with brownouts, a soft translucent volumetric display of slowly turning golden rings and gentle points of light — the last librarian, dimming its own projection to spend watts LISTENING. Around it, the evidence of its ten-thousand-year vigil: a circle swept clean of ash by patient centuries of maintenance, the only tidy place on the planet. Human presence, minimal and reverent: two sets of fresh boot-prints crossing the ash to the swept circle, and a portable coalition power cell placed gently beside the pillar, its cable offered but not yet connected — a gift awaiting permission. Lighting: flat white ash-light, the golden ring-projection as the frame's only warmth. Mood: the loneliest lighthouse in the galaxy — and the day someone finally walked back into its light.
