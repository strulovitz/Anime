Single full-frame exterior masterpiece: the LASER COMM TERMINAL of the mothership Alpha — the thin thread of light that has stayed stretched back to Earth across four light-years and twelve hundred years, because a lifeline you cannot use is still a lifeline. The structure: rising on a slender composite truss mast from the STERN of the ship — mounted aft for the simplest reason in astronomy: Earth is behind them, and always will be — clear of the drive exhausts, standing up out of the shaggy white landscape of lashed ICE-BAGS that armors the six hulls like a machine periscope rising from a glacier. The terminal itself: one generation beyond today's deep-space optical links — a half-meter TELESCOPE in a white thermal shroud, riding a fine two-axis gimbal whose motors hold it steady against the ship's rotation with watchmaker patience; a cluster of STAR TRACKERS at its shoulder fixing its aim to within a whisper of an arcsecond, because at four light-years, missing Earth by one degree is missing it by most of a solar system; a flat phased-array RADIO panel folded alongside as the humble backup; cable runs stitched neatly down the mast with even ties. At this moment, the ship is SPEAKING: from the telescope aperture, a pencil-thin LASER beam runs dead astern toward a single tiny yellow star hanging low among thousands — Sol, marked by absolutely nothing except the beam pointing at it. The physics rendered with total honesty: the beam visible only as a faint, discontinuous scatter-line where it grazes stray frost particles drifting off the ice-bags — a few bright motes catching it like dust in a projector — then effectively INVISIBLE in clean vacuum: a suggestion of a line, not a searchlight; anyone who has stood in a dark lab knows this is exactly how it looks. Below, in soft focus, the six hull sterns recede: the ring of VASIMR nozzles dark and cold, the central fusion exhaust a single faint ember at the spine's end. Background: the full honest starfield, the Milky Way's dust lanes, and Sol — small, yellow, unremarkable, and unbearably far. Lighting: starlight as key, the beam's faint scatter as the only accent, the drive ember as the only warmth. Mood: a lighthouse run in reverse — not guiding ships home, but telling home, every single night for twelve hundred years: still here. Still coming. Still yours.
