#75 — THE MED BAY (the Alpha's quiet mercy)

Single full-frame interior: the MED BAY of the mothership Alpha — the small room that has patched the same ten women through twelve hundred years of sprains, burns, fevers and one another's cooking experiments, mid-morning ship-time, no figures. The space: a compact, immaculate compartment of copper ribs and warm cream paneling — the cleanest room on a ship that is elsewhere lovingly worn, because here cleanliness IS the love. At center: two examination beds of padded off-white, their copper frames polished bright by centuries of gripping hands, one bed neatly made, the other rumpled with a folded blanket and a dent in the pillow — somebody kept overnight for observation and released at breakfast. The working walls: glass-fronted cabinets of instruments arranged with monastic precision, each shelf hand-labeled; a pharmacy cabinet with a heavy brass lock and, taped to its door, a hand-drawn tally chart titled in fading ink "STITCHES — ALL-TIME LEAGUE TABLE" with ten names and centuries of five-bar-gate marks, one name conspicuously in the lead; an autoclave breathing a thread of steam; a dentist's chair in the corner regarded warily by generations, its head-lamp of antique brass. The room's treasure, given a wall of its own: TEN thick medical LOGBOOKS on a shelf, one per crew member, each a tower of volumes bound and re-bound across twelve centuries — the complete health history of ten continuous lives through many bodies, bookmarks and ribbons trailing, the oldest volumes in protective glass. Tender details: a jar of hard candies "FOR BRAVE PATIENTS" with a hand-drawn sunflower; a crutch and a cane in an umbrella stand, both well-used; the caretaker robot's dedicated alcove with a spare fine-manipulator arm racked and sterile — it assists in surgeries; a small window of thick glass with stars, and on its sill a pot of real chamomile from the garden ring. Lighting: clean white key from a surgical lamp, warm amber ambient, steam catching both. Mood: competence as tenderness — the room where "I'm fine" has never once been taken at face value.

#76 — THE SILENT CITY (the Oracle's ash world)

Single full-frame establishing masterpiece, elegiac register: the SILENT CITY — the ten-thousand-year-dead metropolis around the Oracle Node, on a world whose civilization rose, flowered and vanished before humanity smelted bronze, seen from a rubble rise at dead noon under a white ashen sky. The world: grey — volcanic ash has fallen and re-fallen for a hundred centuries, softening everything into a single hushed monochrome, drifts lying against ruins like snow that never melts; a weak white sun, no wind at this hour, the stillness itself the subject. The city: architecture by a vanished species that was NOT humanoid and the ruins say so honestly — no stairs anywhere, only broad spiraling RAMPS; doorways low, wide and triple-arched, sized for bodies broader than tall; buildings of a once-beautiful pale alloy and fused ceramic, now pitted, ash-choked, their upper structures long collapsed, but their proportions still graceful under the burial — a city that was loved by its builders, every ruin a sentence in a language with no readers left. Rendered exactly to canon at the composition's heart, small and precious: the ORACLE NODE — a power-starved, dust-caked projector pillar of pitted pale alloy, waist-high, its tended cables running across the ash to a sad brave array of salvaged, cracked solar panels propped at hopeful angles; above the pillar, dim and FLICKERING with brownouts, a soft translucent volumetric display of slowly turning golden rings and gentle points of light — the last librarian, dimming its own projection to spend watts LISTENING. Around it, the evidence of its ten-thousand-year vigil: a circle swept clean of ash by patient centuries of maintenance, the only tidy place on the planet. Human presence, minimal and reverent: two sets of fresh boot-prints crossing the ash to the swept circle, and a portable coalition power cell placed gently beside the pillar, its cable offered but not yet connected — a gift awaiting permission. Lighting: flat white ash-light, the golden ring-projection as the frame's only warmth. Mood: the loneliest lighthouse in the galaxy — and the day someone finally walked back into its light.

#77 — THE DOMINION SHIPYARD

Single full-frame establishing masterpiece: the DOMINION SHIPYARD — the empire's war-forge in orbit, where the fleets that darken coalition skies are extruded by the thousand, seen from a distance that makes its scale honest, against the grey curve of a strip-mined planet below. The structure: a colossal orbital LATTICE tens of kilometers long — a rigid rectangular scaffold-grid of dark alloy, brutally regular, red beacons blinking in cold unison down its length like a heartbeat with no feeling in it. Within the grid: the production line rendered with industrial honesty — a row of TWENTY identical construction slips, each cradling a Dominion cruiser at a successive stage of assembly, arranged in perfect order of completion like a diagram of birth without childhood: bare keel-spines at one end; then ribbed skeletons swarming with welding drones, their arc-points flickering blue-white; then hulls half-skinned in dark grey plate; and at the line's end, three finished cruisers — angular, symmetrical, sterile — being fueled by umbilical gantries, running lights snapping on section by section in obedient sequence. The logistics of appetite: ore freighters queued in an exact line from the ravaged planet below, its surface visibly scarred with spiral mines and grid-cities; slag and waste vented in glittering plumes that ring the yard in a permanent halo of debris; a mass driver on the lattice's spine firing finished munitions containers toward the front, one every few seconds, metronome-steady. The tell-tale detail, small and cold: the yard has NO habitation ring, NO windows, NO green module anywhere — nothing lives here on purpose; and stenciled on each finished hull, no name — only a serial number. Far foreground, tiny and hidden against a tumbling rock: a matte-black coalition recon probe, watching, counting, its single lens catching the arc-light. Lighting: hard sunlight from frame right, blue-white welding flicker, red beacon rhythm, the planet's grey bounce. Mood: the arithmetic of the enemy — not hatred, worse: indifference at production speed.
