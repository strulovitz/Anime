#60 — THE DOMINION CAPITAL

Single full-frame establishing masterpiece: the DOMINION CAPITAL — the seat of the empire the coalition stands against, a city built to make everyone in it feel small, seen from low aerial distance in hard flat noon light. The architecture: brutal magnificence — colossal towers of dark grey alloy and black glass rising in rigid ranks, every building an exercise in enforced symmetry: stepped ziggurat profiles, knife-straight avenues crossing at perfect angles, no curve anywhere the eye can find, the whole city a circuit-diagram of power. At its center, dominating the skyline: the CITADEL — a single vast obsidian pyramid kilometers wide, its faces polished mirror-black so it wears the sky like armor, ringed at its base by concentric walls and at its peak by a crown of red beacon lights blinking in cold unison. The streets tell the politics: avenues wide enough for parades and nothing else, gigantic banners of dark red hanging hundreds of meters down tower faces, all bearing the same stark angular emblem; patrol craft moving in exact grid lanes, never deviating; and everywhere, at plaza scale, monumental statuary of armored figures with raised arms — dozens of them, all identical, mass-produced triumph. The tell-tale honesty at the margins: down at street level, tiny and almost lost, the actual crowds — dense, grey-clothed, channeled between barriers, dwarfed by their own monuments; and in one narrow gap between towers, a single strip of unauthorized laundry strung between windows, the frame's only warm color. Sky: pale washed-out white, sun a hard disc, thin contrails in parallel lines. Lighting: shadowless institutional noon, red banner accents, mirror-black pyramid reflections. Mood: order as violence — a city with no borscht in it anywhere.

#61 — THE DOMINION LABOR MOON

Single full-frame establishing still: the DOMINION LABOR MOON — the empire's engine room and its crime, where conquered species mine the fuel of Dominion power, seen from a low ridge overlooking the main pit in the grim half-light of a distant sun. The world: airless and grey, stars visible above a black horizon even in "day," the ground churned regolith crusted with vehicle tracks in every direction — none of them going anywhere but down. The pit: a vast open-cast SPIRAL MINE kilometers across, terraced levels corkscrewing into darkness, each level crawling with orange work-lights and the slow glitter of machines; from its depths rises a permanent column of launched ore — an ELECTROMAGNETIC MASS DRIVER on the pit's far rim firing cargo pods skyward in a steady silent rhythm, each pod a brief bright streak toward an orbiting collection station visible as a cold geometric star overhead. The architecture of control rendered honestly: pressurized worker habitats — long half-buried grey tubes in rigid rows, windowless, numbered in giant stencils; guard towers on stilts at measured intervals, red sensor eyes sweeping; a fenced yard of parked exo-suits of MANY DIFFERENT BODY-PLANS — some four-armed, some low and eight-legged, some winged-frames cruelly caged in scaffold — the roll-call of conquered species written in equipment. And the image's quiet heart, foreground on the ridge: half-buried in regolith, a small hand-made CAIRN of stacked stones — a grave, or a marker, or a message — with one tiny piece of colored cloth wired to its top stone, frost-stiff, the only flag on this world not the Dominion's. Lighting: low cold key from the distant sun, orange pit-glow from below, red tower accents. Mood: what the coalition is FOR — the arithmetic of empire, counted in cairns.

#62 — THE THRONE ROOM OF THE DOMINION

Single full-frame interior: the THRONE ROOM at the peak of the Dominion Citadel — the room where the war is decided by one will, empty of its owner, waiting. The space: a vast hall occupying the obsidian pyramid's apex, its four walls leaning inward as they rise, converging high overhead at a single point of harsh white light — the room's only sanctioned sun, dropping one hard column of illumination onto the throne and leaving the hall's edges in engineered darkness. The floor: a single sheet of polished black stone so flawless it doubles the whole room upside-down, a mirror world underfoot. The approach: a long axial walkway flanked by ranks of motionless HONOR-GUARD ARMORS — dozens of identical hollow suits standing at attention in the gloom, red eye-slits dormant, impossible to tell if any are occupied. The throne itself: not ornate — worse: SEVERE — a monolithic seat cut from one block of dark alloy, high-backed, precisely proportioned to be slightly too large for any occupant, so that even power must feel small here; its armrests worn subtly bright in two hand-shaped patches, the room's only trace of a living being. Behind it, filling the entire far wall: the hall's true master — a colossal STRATEGIC DISPLAY, a live map of Dominion space in cold red light, thousands of star-systems tagged and owned, fleet positions crawling — and in its lower corner, small but unignorable, a cluster of systems outlined in defiant GOLD: the rebellion, refusing deletion, the only warm light in the room. Fine details: incense-like coolant mist drifting through the light column; a single data slate abandoned on the throne's armrest; the emblem banners hanging in the dark like sleeping bats. Mood: absolute power, and the small gold stain it cannot scrub out.
