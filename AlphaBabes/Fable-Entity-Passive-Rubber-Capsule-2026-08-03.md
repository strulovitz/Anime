SUBJECT: the PASSIVE VOLCANIC DEPLOYMENT CAPSULE — a sphere-in-sphere rubber-delivery device designed to be lifted by a supervolcano's own gas eruptions and burst open at altitude, raining liquid rubber into the ash plume from within. Reference sheet showing the device in TWO states in one image: deployed flat on the ground (foreground, with cutaway) and airborne ballooned (background). No people.

CAMERA: three-quarter elevated view, 35 mm lens equivalent, foreground capsule large and fully readable, airborne unit small in the upper background sky.

THE CAPSULE (center of the blanket, with cutaway): slung in a woven harness beneath the blanket's center, a silver sphere, 1.5 m diameter, of brushed aluminum with a visible equatorial seam and dogged latches. THE AIRBORNE STATE (background): high in the ash-grey sky, a second identical unit in action — its hexagonal blanket snapped upward into a taut dome like a cross between a drogue parachute and a hot-air balloon, swollen with swallowed volcanic gas, rim weights dangling, the silver sphere hanging beneath it on its harness, riding a pale column of updraft.

DETAILS: stenciled dark markings on the blanket panels (simple pictogram instructions, no readable text); ash dust blowing in thin streams across the ground; one corner of the foreground blanket lifted slightly by wind, showing its underside weave.

FORBIDDEN: no fire touching the foreground capsule, no melting, no fantasy glow; the burst mechanism is pressure-driven, not explosive; materials must read as real fabric, real metal, real aerogel.
