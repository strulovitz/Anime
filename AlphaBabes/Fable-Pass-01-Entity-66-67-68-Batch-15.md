#66 — SUNRISE BASE (the first human settlement on Proxima b)

Single full-frame establishing masterpiece: SUNRISE BASE — the first human settlement on Proxima b, built by ten women at the end of a twelve-hundred-year voyage, seen from a low rise at the eternal dawn of the terminator zone. The setting: Proxima b's twilight belt — the tidally locked world's ring of forever-sunrise, the red dwarf sun sitting permanently swollen and amber-red on the horizon, painting everything in honeyed light; hardy native ground-cover in deep reds and blacks carpeting gentle hills; the day-side glowing warm at one edge of the frame, the night-side a wall of star-strewn indigo at the other. The base: heartbreakingly small and heartbreakingly right — the LANDER at its center, copper and off-white, scorch-feathered, become a building now: legs anchored in poured foundations, its hull grown extensions of local stone and printed panels; around it a ring of exactly TEN small dwellings under construction in varying stages — some finished with warm windows, some still frames — arranged in a circle like the cushions in the observation dome. Between them, the priorities of the ten made visible: the GREENHOUSE finished FIRST — a long copper-framed glass hall glowing green from within, terrestrial crops visible through fogged panes, beets among them; a windbreak wall of stacked local stone, hand-laid, curving protectively around the garden side; a tall mast flying no flag — instead a hand-painted SUNRISE emblem on a copper disc, the same emblem as the EVA suit's shoulder. Details rewarding a close look: cargo crates repurposed as benches around a fire-pit with ten stones set in a circle; boot-paths already worn between greenhouse and lander; a small tilled plot apart from the rest, marked with a copper band — soil begun from the Grave, the old world's circle continued on the new; and high above, a moving star crossing the dawn: the Alpha, in orbit, keeping watch. Mood: arrival — not planting a flag, planting a garden.

#67 — THE SCOUT SHIP (the coalition's little lantern)

Single full-frame hero portrait against deep starfield: a COALITION SCOUT SHIP — the small, brave class of vessel that flies ahead of everyone into places like the Medusa Core, holding station in open space, three-quarter view. The design: the coalition's philosophy in miniature — a compact craft the size of a house, visibly built by MANY HANDS: a stout central hull of human copper-and-steel construction, rivet-seamed and warm; grafted onto it, unmistakably alien contributions honored rather than hidden — a Titanite armor collar of heavy iron plate around the bow, over-engineered and reassuring; a pair of long elegant Aerian sensor vanes sweeping back like folded wings, made of pale lattice-bone and membrane; an Ondine-built water-jacket radiator wrapping the engine section, its coolant channels glowing faint teal through fluted glass. The whole ship a treaty you can fly. Details rewarding a close look: the hull painted with a small SUNRISE emblem beside three alien maker-marks in a proud row; a single warm amber cockpit window at the bow, interior instruments faintly visible, one mug-shaped silhouette on the console shelf; patched micro-impact scars; a folded manipulator arm tucked along the belly holding a sample canister; the drive at rest — two small nozzles breathing the gentle BLUE glow of a banked fusion torch, the "warm blue engine-spark" seen from afar at the Core. Background: distant ember-red wisps of the Orpheus Nebula low in frame, the black of the deep field above. Lighting: cool starlight key, blue drive glow underlighting the stern, amber cockpit warmth. Mood: courage at small scale — the first friendly light dark places ever see, and it's the size of a cottage.

#68 — THE LAUNCH CRADLE (Earth, where the voyage began)

Single full-frame establishing still, historical-memorial register: THE LAUNCH CRADLE on Earth — the place the Alpha left twelve hundred years ago, preserved as it was the morning of departure, seen at dawn in soft grey-gold light. The setting: a high windswept coastal plateau, hard grass bending in a steady sea-wind, the ocean a hammered silver line below, gulls small in a pale sky — Earth rendered ordinary and impossibly precious. The structure: the CRADLE itself — a colossal open framework of weathered steel and copper-toned alloy, a berth shaped in the exact absence of a submarine hull: two kilometer-long rows of curved support arms reaching up around empty air, holding the shape of the ship that is gone, rust-streaked and lichen-spotted now, maintained just enough to stand forever. Between the arms, the scorched launch channel runs out toward the sea, its blast-glazed floor still glassy in patches after twelve centuries, wildflowers growing in the cracks. The memorial honesty: no triumphant statue — instead, at the cradle's head, a modest copper wall bearing TEN names and ten small portraits, young women in their early twenties, the metal around each portrait worn bright by twelve hundred years of visitors' touching fingers; beneath them, a single engraved line and a live, glowing green dot on a small display — the comm laser's latest received message-light, still arriving, four years old each time, never once missed. Details rewarding a close look: offerings left at the wall's base — flowers, hand-written notes under stones, a child's toy rocket, a bowl with a Horezu pattern, a fresh beet placed by someone who knows the stories; a footpath worn deep in the grass, twelve centuries of pilgrimage; the cradle's arms framing, by design, the exact patch of southern sky where Proxima rises. Lighting: dawn grey-gold, sea-light, the green message-dot as the frame's smallest and most important light. Mood: the other end of the lighthouse beam — home, still listening.
