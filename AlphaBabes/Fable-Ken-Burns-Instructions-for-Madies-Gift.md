First, one tiny bit of beginner theory (only this once — future scenes will be short):

    Ken Burns = 2 keyframes. In Premiere you set Scale (zoom) and Position at the start of the clip, and again at the end. Premiere animates smoothly between them. That's all it is. Rule of thumb: keep movements small and slow — a change of 15–25% in scale over the whole clip is plenty. Big fast moves look cheap; tiny slow moves look cinematic.

🎥 SCENE 1 — The Alpha in the Void — Ken Burns

Director's intent: We are drifting in space, slowly approaching the ship — like a small observation pod gliding toward the majestic Alpha. The movement should feel weightless and patient, matching the hushed narration.
Setting	Start of clip	End of clip
Scale (zoom)	100%	118%
Position	Centered — whole ship visible	Drift slightly toward the bow dish (the big geodesic dish catching the red starlight)

    Motion in one sentence: Slow, constant zoom IN from the full wide shot toward the great dish glowing in Proxima's red light.
    Duration: Stretch the movement across the entire narration audio (about 35–40 seconds) — do not finish the zoom early.
    Smoothing: Right-click each keyframe → Ease Out (on the first) and Ease In (on the last). This makes the motion start and stop gently instead of mechanically.
    Why this motion: The narration ends on "toward a small red star... called Proxima Centauri" — so the camera physically travels toward the dish that drinks the light of that star. Audio and image arrive together. 🎯

What I did:
