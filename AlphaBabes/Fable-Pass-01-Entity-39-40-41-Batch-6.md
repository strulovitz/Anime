#39 — KEPLER-442b

Single full-frame establishing masterpiece: the surface of KEPLER-442b, homeworld of the Zĭrán, seen from a high forest ridge in the honeyed amber daylight of its orange dwarf sun — a whole world lit like a permanent late afternoon. The land tells its physics: under 1.3 times Earth's gravity, the forests grow BROAD instead of tall — immense thick-trunked trees with wide, low, layered canopies of indigo-black foliage (dark pigments evolved to drink the orange star's light), spreading like banked storm clouds of leaves across the valley below, with ribbons of white mist threading the hollows between them. The denser air renders distance in soft luminous haze: on the valley floor, a river flashes amber; in the middle distance, the TREE CITY rises from the canopy like a living cathedral — a cluster of colossal grown towers, tiny grown windows glinting, threads of teal lamplight just visible along its bridges; and on the far horizon, serene and vast, the snow-capped cone of MT. JÙN, the great dormant volcano, wrapped in evening cloud at its shoulders. The sky: deep warm gold at the horizon graduating to teal-blue at the zenith, where TWO small silvery moons hang together in the daylight, one gibbous, one crescent. Life in the air: a loose flock of domesticated air-jellyfish drifting above the canopy like translucent lanterns, and drifting seed-motes catching the sun. Lighting: low golden key from the orange sun, long soft shadows across the canopy, haze scattering the light. Mood: a world at peace with itself — abundance, patience, and amber warmth from horizon to horizon.

#40 — THE RUINED PLAZA OF WOLF 1061c

Single full-frame establishing still: the dead plaza of WOLF 1061c — a civilization that fought Medusa ten thousand years ago and lost, preserved in the silence it left behind. The sky: bruised orange-purple, sunless-feeling, heavy with thin high haze. Everything below it is dusted in fine grey ASH that softens every edge like ten millennia of snow — and the ash is UNDISTURBED: not one footprint, not one track, anywhere. The plaza: once magnificent — a broad ceremonial square of pale cracked pavement, ringed by the shattered stumps of elegant towers whose surviving fragments still show graceful non-human architecture: flowing curves, no right angles, silhouetted black against the bruised sky. In the middle distance, a defense turret slumped like a melted candle, its barrel drooped and re-solidified mid-drip, glassy where the heat ran. Across the foreground, the image's heart: a toppled monument — a graceful NON-HUMANOID figure of flowing multi-limbed form, carved in pale stone, lying full-length across the cracked pavement, ash pooled in the curves of its face. And on the one great wall still standing behind it, the quietest horror: pale SCORCHED SILHOUETTES burned permanently into the stone where people once stood — several scattered figures, and near the wall's center two silhouettes close together, limbs entwined. No bodies anywhere: absence as the only ruin that matters. Lighting: flat dim overcast key, faint warm horizon glow, no strong shadows — a world with the contrast burned out of it. Mood: reverence and warning; the silence Madie's coalition exists to answer.

#41 — THE REBEL ASTEROID BASE

Single full-frame interior: the GREAT HALL of the hidden rebel base, carved inside a hollow asteroid — the room where the first Council of Species will meet. The space: a vast rough chamber of raw grey rock, walls still bearing the long curved scars of the mining machines that hollowed it, the ceiling ribbed with warm COPPER girders bolted directly into the stone. Light: strings of jury-rigged fusion lamps hung on long looped cables between the girders, pooling warm practical light over a floor of scuffed deck plates laid unevenly across the rock; crates and cable-drums serve as furniture along the walls. At the exact center, the room's heart and key light: a large round HOLO-TABLE — raw rock pedestal, copper-framed projector ring — casting a slowly rotating three-dimensional star map of Dominion space in luminous GOLD above itself, hundreds of tiny marked stars and thread-thin route lines, the gold light washing the nearest rock walls. Around the table, hospitality written as engineering — five kinds of seating, none matching: worn human chairs; broad low stone floor-pads for eight-legged Titanites; a tall open PERCH-RAIL of copper pipe for winged Aerians; a wheeled transparent WATER-ALCOVE, softly glowing teal, its sea gently rippling, ready for an Ondine; and one wide six-lobed woven floor cushion shaped for a hexapod Zĭrán. Small honest details: a kettle steaming on a crate, hand-chalked tallies on a rock wall, a patched pressure door. No figures — the room waiting for history. Mood: improvised, warm, defiant — a cave that decided to become a parliament.
